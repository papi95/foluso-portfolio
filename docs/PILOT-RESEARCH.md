# Pilot research kit

## Recruitment

Interview 15–20 people before opening the pilot: producers/hiring managers, musicians, live/AV crew, photographers/videographers, editors/designers, and freelancers. Start with Lagos-based participants plus a small number of remote collaborators. Recruit across experience levels. Ask for consent before taking notes; do not record without explicit consent.

## 20-minute interview script

1. Tell me about the last media gig you found or staffed. How did it happen?
2. What took the most time or created the most uncertainty?
3. Where do you currently look for crew, work, or specialist help?
4. What makes you trust or distrust a gig post or a new collaborator?
5. What information must a gig include before you apply or book someone?
6. How do rates, payment timing, travel, and cancellations work in your current process?
7. What would make you report a post or member? What response would you expect?
8. Which matters most to you for a first version: find crew, post gigs, equipment, or peer advice? Why?
9. Looking at the prototype, what would you try first? What is unclear or missing?
10. What is the smallest useful service you would try in the next month?

Do not lead with feature pitches. Ask for recent behavior and concrete examples; separate what people say they might use from what they have done or agreed to test.

## Prototype tasks

- Find a camera-operator gig in Lagos.
- Post a clearly scoped paid opportunity.
- Apply to a role with a short experience note.
- Find where to report a suspicious or unsafe post.

Observe completion and hesitation. Do not ask participants to enter real passwords, payment details, or sensitive personal data during research.

## Interview notes (minimum necessary)

Record participant role, broad experience band, task outcome, friction, trust concerns, and follow-up permission. Use a participant code instead of a name in synthesis. Store notes in a restricted folder and delete raw notes when no longer needed. Ask separately before quoting or attributing feedback publicly.

## Synthesis

After each five interviews, group repeated problems and rank by frequency, severity, and how well the current gig-board MVP can address them. Change the roadmap only when evidence supports it. Contact participants only with the follow-up they agreed to receive.
